At the heart of this tragedy lies the acute shortage of official housing options provided by educational institutions. Who Holds the Reins of Responsibility?

Blame in such complex systems is multifaceted. Landlords operate under pressure to maximize profit, frequently at the expense of safety and legality — their primary failure lies in skirting the regulations that safeguard residents.

But universities like Delhi University share a slice of the accountability, too. The chronic under-provision of student housing forces students into precarious situations, perpetuating demand for unregulated accommodation. The responsibility to expand safe living quarters should paramountly reside with these institutions.

City regulators bear their own share of the onus, to enforce building safety norms rigorously. The leniency historically shown by regulatory bodies effectively permits landlords to exploit weak enforcement, prioritizing economic activity over civil safety.

Pathways to Prevention

Preventing future disasters demands coordinated action from multiple fronts:

  • Stricter regulation and enforcement: city regulators need more stringent monitoring and frequent inspections to ensure adherence to safety standards.
  • University expansion of housing: universities must prioritize expanding official hostels; public-private partnerships could hasten construction of affordable, secure student housing.

Ultimately, the solution lies in addressing the root cause: the housing shortage itself. Concerted effort from government, city planners, and educational institutions is essential.
